Where does the iris color come from?

MediaPipe locates the iris; our pixel sampler estimates its visible color. We sample a ring around the center, aiming to skip the pupil, lids, dark shadows, and bright reflections. The swatch is a camera-color estimate, not a measurement of natural pigmentation. Lighting, white balance, contact lenses, and a dilated pupil can change it. Come closer and use soft front lighting; compare the swatch with what you see.

How do these signals become a blink?

The gap is the distance between the upper and lower lid samples, measured relative to eye width and compared with your own open-eye gap. MediaPipe’s blink score supplies another clue; it is not a confidence percentage. The two are blended into one smoothed openness value. Closing is immediate, but about 120 ms of steady open evidence is needed to reopen, so a held wink does not flicker. Signals can disagree, so “uncertain” is useful feedback. The iris estimate is retained during closure, but it should not steer gaze.

Everything here uses anatomical left and right. Only the pictures are mirrored. The playful eyes also apply smoothing and optional echo; these inspection readings do not.
